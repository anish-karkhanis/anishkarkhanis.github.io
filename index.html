Adult obesity trend modelling for NHS Public Health Lanarkshire

The case

Scotland's Population Health Framework 2025–2035 named healthy weight an immediate priority, but NHS Lanarkshire, serving around 655,000 adults in one of Scotland's most deprived regions, had no locally calibrated model to define what achieving that would require, or to test whether current trends were anywhere near sufficient.

What I did
  • Analysed 12 years of Health Board prevalence data and ran a formal linear regression test for trend.
  • Rejected ARIMA on statistical grounds, since overlapping four-year reporting windows left 9 nominal observations equivalent to fewer than three independent ones.
  • Built 4 deterministic scenarios to 2035, deriving the GLP-1 contribution bottom-up from the eligible patient population rather than an assumed uptake rate.
  • Ran sensitivity analysis and used Scotland-level SIMD data as proxy, since Lanarkshire deprivation splits are suppressed.
Findings
  • Prevalence has sat flat between 32% and 34% since 2012 with no statistically detectable trend (p = 0.28), and the most recent figure of 34% is the highest in the series.
  • Framework-aligned action at 0.3 points a year reaches only 30.7% by 2035, well short of the 28% indicative benchmark.
  • Hitting the benchmark implies a required rate of 0.545 percentage points a year.
  • Realistic GLP-1 contribution at current eligibility is roughly 0.03 points a year, an order of magnitude below what the accelerated scenario assumes.
Recommendations

Six recommendations, led by confirming the operative benchmark with the Board before anything else, since every downstream figure is conditional on it. Adopt 0.545 points a year as a monitoring floor rather than a target, plan delivery at 0.6 or above, prepare for staged expansion of GLP-1 eligibility, and introduce deprivation-stratified monitoring so aggregate progress cannot mask a widening inequality gap. Model scripts were handed over so the Board can rerun it annually.

NHS A&E pressure management: forecasting and resource planning

The case

England's A&E departments now run at a permanently higher baseline, with monthly attendances above 2.2 million since 2022, yet planning frameworks still treat that level as a winter peak. The brief was to turn NHS England attendance data into forecasts and prioritised staffing decisions that planning teams could act on.

What we did
  • Analysed national A&E attendance data from April 2020 to January 2026 (about 70 monthly observations) and benchmarked four-hour performance across ICBs.
  • Built a Random Forest forecasting model on a temporal train/test split to avoid data leakage, with a COVID-period dummy variable to handle the structural break.
  • Ranked ICBs with multi-criteria decision analysis (attendances 60%, four-hour performance 40%) and tested three scenarios for demand and staffing.
Findings
  • Test set R² of 0.94 and a mean error of around 4.1%, with the model smoothing winter peaks, so planning should use the upper forecast range.
  • A 12–20 percentage point four-hour compliance gap between top and bottom ICBs that demand differences alone could not explain.
  • Greater Manchester, Cheshire and Merseyside, and Cumbria and North East scored above the 0.70 high-priority threshold.
  • A winter surge of +15% implied 45–50 additional WTE per top-decile ICB for two months, at roughly £1.8–2.0M per ICB.
Recommendations

A three-stage roadmap: pre-agree bank and agency staffing at the three priority ICBs before the February and March peak, commission benchmarking partnerships pairing bottom-decile ICBs with the best performers, and move to ICB-level probabilistic forecasting so plans can be set against risk-adjusted ranges rather than single-point estimates.

Energy efficiency and operational improvement at WB Alloys

The case

A metals manufacturer needed to reduce energy consumption and improve operational efficiency, but had no basis for deciding which interventions were worth the capital and disruption, or in what order to attempt them.

What I did
  • Assessed the feasibility of 5 distinct efficiency interventions across the operation.
  • Weighed each on impact against implementation feasibility rather than raw saving alone.
  • Sequenced the viable ones into a phased roadmap aligned to the ISO 50001 energy management standard.
Findings
  • All 5 interventions were viable, but their payback profiles differed enough that ordering materially changed the return.
  • Phasing across 0–18 months allowed quick wins to part-fund the heavier capital items later in the sequence.
Recommendations

Adopt the phased ISO 50001-aligned roadmap over an eighteen month horizon, front-loading the low-disruption interventions, with projected savings of £3,900 to £4,500 across a three year period.

Growth strategy for CorVista Health

The case

CorVista Health, a specialty EMR vendor serving cardiology practices, was approaching saturation in its core market. The brief asked which of two expansion paths to take: move into analytics, or move into cardiovascular surgery.

What I did
  • Assessed competitive position and mapped where a defensible advantage actually sat, rather than where revenue currently came from.
  • Evaluated both expansion paths on addressable market, time to revenue and competitive exposure.
  • Built a sequenced strategy rather than an either-or answer, with the revenue build modelled to Year 5.
Findings
  • The EMR product itself was not the moat, the cardiovascular encounter data held inside it was, and no competitor could replicate that quickly.
  • That data was generating $0 in analytics revenue despite being the most defensible asset on the balance sheet.
  • Entering surgery cold meant competing without the hospital relationships that make the entry viable.
Recommendations

Monetise the encounter data first, building an analytics platform on an asset already owned and paid for. Use the resulting platform and the hospital relationships it deepens as a wedge into cardiovascular surgery second, rather than attempting that entry from a standing start. Sequenced this way the strategy builds toward $44M in ARR by Year 5, turning what was effectively a dormant data liability into the growth engine.
